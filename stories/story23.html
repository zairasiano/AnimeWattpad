Midnight at the Museum

By Nene Kisaragi

A young curator investigates an exhibit that changes every night.

Chapter 1: The Changing Exhibit

Nene had recently started working as a junior curator at the city's oldest museum. She loved studying ancient objects and learning the stories behind them.

One evening, she stayed late to prepare a new exhibit. Before leaving, she checked every display case and turned off the lights in the main gallery.

The next morning, Nene returned to find something unusual. A small golden compass had appeared inside a display case that had been empty the night before.

She checked the museum records, but there was no information about the compass. The security cameras showed no one entering the gallery overnight.

Curious, Nene decided to inspect the exhibit after closing time. She hid in the office and watched the gallery clock approach midnight.

As the clock struck twelve, the lights flickered. A painting on the far wall slowly changed, revealing a picture of the museum's entrance covered in snow.

Nene stepped closer. The golden compass began to spin inside its case, pointing toward a narrow hallway that was not marked on the museum's floor plan.

She followed the hallway and discovered a locked wooden door. A message had been carved into its surface: "Every night, history changes. Every morning, someone forgets."

Nene took out her notebook and recorded everything she had seen. Before she could leave, the compass stopped spinning and pointed directly at her.
